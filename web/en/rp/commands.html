GTA RP commands: /me and /do with examples

What is your character doing? What can others observe? When should chance decide? Compare the commands, then follow a short scene from start to finish.

Commands

/me

Describe what your character does

/me adds details that an animation cannot show: small movements, handling an object or the way your character performs an action. You describe your own actions, not guaranteed success or another character’s response.

How to use it

Write briefly in the third person. When an action affects another character, describe the attempt and leave room for their player to respond. You do not always need to narrate actions already shown by game mechanics; follow your community’s rules.

ROLEPLAY EXAMPLE

/me takes a crumpled note from their pocket and slides it across the table towards Ethan.

/me reaches towards Ethan’s wrist and attempts to catch it.

Common mistake

“/me knocks Ethan down and leaves him unable to move” decides the other player’s outcome for them. Avoid invisible thoughts such as “/me thinks about revenge”; describe observable behaviour instead.

Server-specific details

These examples illustrate text roleplay, not a replacement for a server’s combat mechanics or interaction rules.

Commands

/do

Describe a scene or ask about its details

/do adds observable circumstances: an object’s appearance, marks on clothing or details that can be discovered in a scene. It can also ask a question when your character examines something and another player knows the answer.

How to use it

The distinction is simple: /me describes an action; /do describes a state or finding. Answer truthfully according to the established scene, but only about what the action could reveal. Do not invent another player’s inventory or facts you could not verify.

ROLEPLAY EXAMPLE

Ethan: /me turns the note under the light and examines its reverse side.

Ethan: /do Is anything written on the back?

Olivia: /do A garage address is written on the back in blue ink.

Common mistake

“/do Olivia feels scared and immediately confesses” dictates someone else’s emotions and decision. /do is not mind reading. A character may lie in dialogue, but do not misrepresent the actual scene through /do.

Server-specific details

Your server determines the scope of questions and required responses. Asking a question does not let you skip a search, consent or a game mechanic.

Commands

/try

Use chance for an uncertain attempt

On servers that support it, /try generates a random outcome, often yes or no. It can resolve a small uncertainty in a scene when no game mechanic already determines the result and the rules allow its use.

How to use it

First establish what your character is attempting and what each outcome means. If another player is affected, agree on a process that follows the rules. Chance does not replace character abilities or make an impossible action plausible.

ROLEPLAY EXAMPLE

Situation: Ethan tests an old lighter; the community allows /try for this kind of minor uncertainty.

If /try text syntax is supported: /try manages to light the old lighter on the first attempt.

Work the result into the scene: on failure, the flame does not catch and Ethan must respond another way.

Common mistake

Do not repeat the command until you get a success. Do not use it to automatically injure another player, bypass a lock or decide someone else’s death without support from the rules.

Server-specific details

Syntax, output and permitted situations vary. Some servers do not offer /try or restrict when it can be used.

Commands

/doc

A counter for longer roleplay actions

Some servers use /doc to count during an action that should not happen instantly. It accompanies descriptions through /me and /do; numbers alone do not explain what your character is doing or establish the result.

How to use it

Describe the action first, then start the counter if your server supports it. After it finishes, describe an outcome consistent with the scene. If someone interrupts the action, respond to the interruption — a counter does not guarantee completion.

ROLEPLAY EXAMPLE

/me takes a needle and thread and begins stitching the torn sleeve of their own jacket.

/doc 10

/do A rough, visible stitch holds the torn section of the sleeve together.

Common mistake

Do not assume /doc 10 means ten seconds on every server. A counter does not repair a vehicle, heal an injury or replace a required item or game mechanic.

Server-specific details

The /doc 10 example applies only where that syntax is supported. Check your server’s documentation for the interval, counting direction and limits.

Commands

/report

Ask the server staff for help

On servers that offer it, /report contacts administrators out of character. Use it for a technical issue or a situation that the rules assign to the staff team. It is not an in-character call to the police or an ambulance.

How to use it

Explain what happened, where you are and what help you need. When reporting a rule violation, include any available player ID, the time and evidence through the designated process. Stick to facts, not insults. Do not disclose personal information.

ROLEPLAY EXAMPLE

If the server accepts text after the command: /report After loading the garage interior, I became stuck in the floor. I cannot move; could someone help?

For a dispute, describe the sequence of events and prepare a recording if required; staff determine how to submit it.

Common mistake

Avoid messages such as “admin come here” and do not repeatedly send the same report. Filing a report does not automatically allow you to stop roleplaying or disconnect from the scene.

Server-specific details

The command may open a form, accept text or be replaced by a ticket system. Follow the community’s process and the administrator’s instructions.

What does a shared RP scene look like?

Ethan brings a jacket with a torn sleeve to Olivia’s workshop. This is an example of text roleplay; adapt the commands and process to your server’s rules.

  1. 01 · ACTION

    Ethan: /me places the jacket on the counter and turns the torn sleeve towards Olivia.

  2. 02 · QUESTION

    Olivia: /do Is the fabric only torn, or is a piece missing as well?

  3. 03 · ANSWER

    Ethan: /do There is a short tear in the sleeve. No fabric is missing.

  4. 04 · RESPONSE

    Olivia: /me examines the edges of the tear and prepares a needle and thread.

  5. 05 · PROGRESS

    Olivia: /doc 10 — only if the server supports this command and syntax.

  6. 06 · RESULT

    Olivia: /do A visible stitch closes the tear; the sleeve can be used again.

/me advances the action, /do adds facts and /doc can represent time spent on it. /try is unnecessary here: a scene does not need every command. /report is for asking the server staff for help.

These practices come from existing RP communities. They are not a confirmed GTA VI command list. Availability, syntax and rules depend on the server.
