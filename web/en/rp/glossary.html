Basics

RP

Roleplay

Playing a fictional character in a shared world. Make decisions based on their knowledge, personality and circumstances, rather than simply what benefits you as a player.

RP EXAMPLE

Your character is afraid of heights, so they hesitate during a rooftop escape — even though you know you could make the jump in the game.

Basics

IC

In Character

Everything that happens within the character and game world: conversations, experiences, information and decisions.

RP EXAMPLE

A conversation with a mechanic about a broken-down van is IC communication.

More about IC and OOC
Basics

OOC

Out of Character

Communication and information outside the character. This includes technical issues, agreements between players and resolving a situation with an administrator.

RP EXAMPLE

“My microphone isn't working” belongs in the designated OOC channel, not in your character's story.

Rules

What is metagaming in RP?

Bringing OOC knowledge into IC

Using information you know as a player that your character hasn't learned in the game. It can undermine fair play and the story.

RP EXAMPLE

You learn another player's location from a stream and send your character there, even though they couldn't know where to go.

More about IC and OOC

How to recognize it

The source of the information matters. Seeing Ethan’s location on a stream does not make it known to your character. Before acting on information, ask when and how your character learned it. If you cannot answer, do not use it to guide their decision.

Rules

What is powergaming in RP?

Forcing an outcome

Forcing an action or outcome without giving another player a reasonable chance to respond, or giving a character unrealistic abilities. The server sets the exact definition.

RP EXAMPLE

Instead of automatically saying “you tie him up and carry him away,” describe an attempt and let the other player respond under the server's rules.

How to recognize it

In a text interaction, distinguish an attempt from a forced outcome. “Attempts to grab Ethan’s wrist” leaves room for a response. “Breaks Ethan’s arm and leaves him unable to react” decides the other player’s outcome. Even plausible actions must follow server rules and mechanics.

Rules

What does RDM mean in RP?

Random Deathmatch

Attacking or killing another character without a sufficient RP reason. The server's rules define when conflict is allowed.

RP EXAMPLE

Shooting a random passerby out of boredom isn't a meaningful RP situation.

How to recognize it

RDM stands for Random Deathmatch. A typical example is attacking an unfamiliar character simply because they walk past. A disagreement does not automatically justify shooting. A server may require prior interaction and proportionate escalation.

Rules

What does VDM mean in RP?

Vehicle Deathmatch

Using a vehicle to attack other characters without a valid reason. Exceptions for story situations vary between servers.

RP EXAMPLE

Deliberately driving into a crowd without a story-based reason.

How to recognize it

VDM stands for Vehicle Deathmatch. It involves using a vehicle to attack without an adequate roleplay reason. An accidental collision is not automatically VDM: intent, context and rules matter. The consequences of the collision still belong in the scene.

Rules

What is Fear RP?

Fear and self-preservation

Your character should take threats and their own life seriously. This doesn't mean every situation has one mandatory response — follow the server's rules.

RP EXAMPLE

An unarmed character surrounded by armed people considers cooperating, retreating or negotiating.

How to recognize it

Fear RP means portraying a believable concern for your character’s life and safety. A brave character can still feel fear. Consider the threat they can actually observe and their available options; there is no universal attacker or weapon count that determines every response.

Rules

What is Fail RP?

Breaking believability

Behavior that contradicts the rules or logic of the RP world. This is a broad term, and interpretations can vary.

RP EXAMPLE

After a serious crash, a character runs off to carry on working without reacting to their injuries.

How to recognize it

Fail RP is a broad term for behaviour that breaks the plausibility or rules of roleplay. Ignoring a serious injury and continuing as though nothing happened can be an example. A failed attempt or an unusual character personality is not automatically Fail RP.

Rules

Combat logging

Disconnecting to avoid consequences

Intentionally disconnecting during a scene to escape its consequences. If your game crashes, follow the server's procedure and report the technical issue.

RP EXAMPLE

Logging out during an arrest just to avoid losing your items.

Server

Whitelist

Approved server access

A system requiring an application, interview or another check before a player can join. It doesn't automatically guarantee a high-quality server.

RP EXAMPLE

Before joining, you submit a character backstory and explain the basic rules.

Server

Faction

An organized group of characters

A group within the game world with its own roles and relationships, such as police, paramedics, a business or a story-driven group.

RP EXAMPLE

A garage has an owner, mechanics and its own work routine.

Character

Lore

The background of a world or character

The events, relationships and circumstances that explain who a character is and why they act as they do. Their lore must fit the server's world.

RP EXAMPLE

A former cook moves closer to his sister and hopes to open a small bistro.

How to write character lore
Character

CK

Character Kill

Permanently ending a character's story. The specific server determines the conditions, consent and consequences; never assume them automatically.

RP EXAMPLE

An agreed ending to a long story, following the server's approved procedure.

Character

PK

Player Kill / consequences of death

A term used for a non-permanent death or the consequences of a character being incapacitated. Memory, return and injury rules vary widely between servers.

RP EXAMPLE

Before continuing after revival, check what your character is allowed to remember.

Commands

/me

Describe what your character does

/me adds details that an animation cannot show: small movements, handling an object or the way your character performs an action. You describe your own actions, not guaranteed success or another character’s response.

Commands

/do

Describe a scene or ask about its details

/do adds observable circumstances: an object’s appearance, marks on clothing or details that can be discovered in a scene. It can also ask a question when your character examines something and another player knows the answer.

Commands

/try

Use chance for an uncertain attempt

On servers that support it, /try generates a random outcome, often yes or no. It can resolve a small uncertainty in a scene when no game mechanic already determines the result and the rules allow its use.

Commands

/doc

A counter for longer roleplay actions

Some servers use /doc to count during an action that should not happen instantly. It accompanies descriptions through /me and /do; numbers alone do not explain what your character is doing or establish the result.

Commands

/report

Ask the server staff for help

On servers that offer it, /report contacts administrators out of character. Use it for a technical issue or a situation that the rules assign to the staff team. It is not an in-character call to the police or an ambulance.

Rules

Mixing

Mixing IC and OOC

Bringing out-of-character communication into a scene. Keep player conversations separate from what the characters say.

RP EXAMPLE

A character talks about a keyboard during an arrest instead of using the designated OOC channel.

Rules

Passive RP

A world beyond the visible players

Account for residents, staff and city life even where the game doesn't show them. The community sets the details.

RP EXAMPLE

An empty hospital corridor doesn't mean the entire hospital is unstaffed.

Rules

NonRP Injuries

Ignoring injuries

Failing to acknowledge injuries and their consequences in a character's story. The server sets treatment and limitations.

RP EXAMPLE

A character immediately sprints after a serious crash without reacting to any injury.

Rules

What does NLR mean in RP?

New Life Rule

Rules about memory and returning after death or respawning. Their scope, exceptions and time limits aren't universal.

RP EXAMPLE

Before returning to a scene, check what your character remembers and whether they're allowed to go back.

How to recognize it

NLR stands for New Life Rule. Before continuing after death or respawn, check what your character may remember, whether they can return and whether a waiting period applies. Do not assume a universal time limit. NLR is not automatically the same as permanent character death, or CK.

Rules

Cop Baiting

Provoking police without a valid reason

Provoking police solely to start a chase or conflict, without an adequate story-based reason.

RP EXAMPLE

Repeatedly circling a patrol and deliberately provoking officers just to make them chase you.

Rules

NonRP Driving

Unbelievable behavior behind the wheel

Driving that ignores the vehicle's capabilities and the consequences of crashes within the RP world.

RP EXAMPLE

A sports car races across rocky terrain at full speed and carries on without reacting to a serious impact.

Metagaming vs powergaming: what is the difference?

Metagaming concerns information your character should not have. Powergaming concerns forced outcomes or implausible actions. Using a stream to locate a player is metagaming; declaring that their character cannot resist your action can be powergaming. Both can occur in the same scene.

Sources and use of terms
